Its latest feature is an AI Wiki where technicians store step-by-step procedures; in a later phase, AI agents would use those procedures to answer work-order help requests. The prototype worked, but it was built for people who write like engineers. As the only designer, I audited it across four core flows, found 15 UX gaps, and redesigned each flow around two users: a technician writing at a GED reading level, and an AI that needs structure to answer from. Six of my redesigns have shipped."

<Section id="users" index="02" label="Users" title="Users">
  <div class="grid max-w-[65ch] gap-4 text-lg leading-relaxed text-muted">
    <p>Everyone in this chain uses the wiki: the technician who writes a procedure, the lead who reviews it, the admin who decides who sees it, the requester who needs help. Many of them have a GED-level education and spend most of their day on site, not at a computer.</p>
    <p>That was the barrier this project set out to remove. If any step feels like paperwork, knowledge stops moving, and in phase 2 the AI agent has nothing reliable to answer from.</p>
