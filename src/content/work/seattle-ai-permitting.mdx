Our team started at the City’s permitting hackathon and designed AI help for three steps of the application: the timeline, the form and a review before submitting. In UW’s HCDE 516 we then tested how much AI those steps need. We built three versions with no AI, partial AI and full AI and ran a study with 42 architects and residents. Partial AI raised usability by 34% and trust by 39%. Full AI added no significant gain on top."

<Section id="outcome" index="06" label="Outcome" title="Outcome">
  <Statement>The version we designed first <em>wasn’t the one to build.</em></Statement>
  <p class="max-w-[680px] text-lg leading-[1.6] text-muted">We designed the full-AI version first. Partial AI at the same three steps got most of the gain. Personalization and cited sources add build cost for no significant gain. If the City builds one, partial AI is the level to start with.</p>
</Section>

<Section id="takeaways" index="07" label="Takeaways" title="Takeaways">
  <ol>
    <Takeaway n={1} title="The right amount of AI wasn’t the most AI">Going in, more AI looked like the obvious answer. The data showed partial AI captured most of the gain. Now I test how much help a step needs before adding more.</Takeaway>
    <Takeaway n={2} title="Change one thing at a time">All three versions ran the same three steps. Only the AI level changed. That is what made the result readable.</Takeaway>
    <Takeaway n={3} title="What this study can’t tell us">Each version had only 4 architects, and we couldn’t recruit City permit reviewers. Partial and full AI were also hard to tell apart for some participants. A next round would use real applications and measure time and errors.</Takeaway>
  </ol>
